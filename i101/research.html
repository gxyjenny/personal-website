Research


Having research provides a solid foundation to ensure that solutions have support and are viable. In order to gain comprehensive knowledge, it’s best to look through every perspective of the problem to ensure a complete solution. Through research pertaining to farmers markets, we are able to gain a solid understanding of what the market is like, as well as its competition and any other problems that are resulting in its decline. Through technical research, we are able to piece together a market strategy in order to bring the app to launch. Understanding the market of these apps is also helpful to successfully launch an app that best appeals to its users and demographics.

Local food purchasing has proved to economically provide productive use of land, spark small business growth, and gives consumers an alternative choice of quality variances of food. A lot of economic as well as social benefits come with local food sales, including even possible reductions of gas emissions. The demand is there with 87.2% of consumers considering local farm food “very/somewhat important” and with consumers more than ever caring what they eat and where it comes from (Tropp, “Why Local Food Matters”). But the market for direct-to-consumer sales only breaches 18% of the economic sales, and accessibility has been an ongoing problem with lack of distribution and weak marketing infrastructure (Tropp, “Why Local Food Matters”). Companies have taken alternative routes to expand in this growing sector, especially with food hub/retail partnerships. Although accessibility has been a major con, farmer stands have been growing gradually. Advances in farm to school programs, farmer market operations in healthcare facilities, and new health initiatives in military bases will only serve as a catalyst for future demand. It will be interesting seeing how local markets will gain better accessibility.

Due to competition not only from competing farmer markets, but from retailers like Whole Foods, it’s much harder for farmer markets to retain their customers (Carman, “For some growers, farmers markets just aren’t what they used to be”). Farmer markets have become a much more social experience, with the focus on food and drinks instead of the fresh, organic produce. In order to retain customers, farmer markets need to appeal to the demographic shift. By incorporating technology, a mobile app will help the retention as the market has shifted to a younger, more technologically advanced demographic.

With the recent exponential growth of mobile apps available, many companies have chosen to bypass “traditional methods of development, marketing, and distribution in favor of the new online app stores run by Apple, Google, and few other software and communications giants” (Anthes, “Invasion of the Mobile Apps”). Companies that choose to do this depend on their app garnering attention through the app store instead of using traditional marketing strategies. The low cost of production and upkeep combined with the ease of launching apps has caused a major uptick in various apps published by countless developers. Many apps launch without any marketing strategy, so in order to ensure success, it would be extremely beneficial for the farmers market app to have a market strategy.

In order to successfully make and implement a farmers market app, there are certain factors to be aware of such as user retention. It’s vital to not only engage our users, but to retain them to continue using the app. Making an app user friendly will help this, as well as promoting the app to peak users’ interests. Getting feedback from users will also ensure that the app continues to develop down the right path. Another factor to keep in mind is our online presence. If we make an app, we may want to make a website or a blog to keep up our reputation. (Gauchet, “The Ultimate iPhone App Marketing Strategy Guide”). By taking account of these factors, the app will have a much higher chance of being successful and one that users continuously use.

Based off of a survey we conducted, the results aligned with our project plan for the farmers market app. The majority of responses agreed that a mobile app would be the best solution, and it would encourage users to go to farmers markets more (Farmers Market Team, “Farmers Market Survey”). With the survey results as well as the research conducted through the various sources, our team is more confident that the project plan for the mobile app is the best solution going forward. With the mobile app marketing strategies and information about the current state for farmers markets, the app will not only attract users, but continue to engage them as the demographic for farmer markets become more technological.
